Great question. Standard open source licenses (MIT, GPL, Apache) are amazing for libraries and software tools. But they all explicitly allow commercial use and resale – that's part of the Open Source Definition.

AVI is different. We're a non-profit academic initiative. We spend our time building templates for scholars, not for resellers. If we used MIT, someone could legally:

  • Download all our free templates
  • Bundle them into a paid "Academic Website Kit" on ThemeForest
  • Sell them for $49 each
  • And we couldn't stop them

So we chose a source-available, non-commercial license instead. It protects our mission while still giving scholars full freedom to use, modify, and share the templates. No. The ShareAlike (SA) clause requires derivative works to use the same non-commercial license.

It's a Creative Commons license with three conditions:

  • BY – Attribution: Credit the original creator
  • NC – Non-Commercial: No commercial use allowed
  • SA – ShareAlike: Derivative works must use same license

The "4.0" refers to the version (latest, released 2013).

ShareAlike means if you modify or build upon the material, you must license your new work under the same CC BY-NC-SA license.

Example:

  • You download an AVI template (CC BY-NC-SA)
  • You heavily customize it (colors, layout, new sections)
  • You share your customized version
  • You must license your customized version under CC BY-NC-SA

This is often called a "viral" or "copyleft" clause — it ensures the work stays free and non-commercial forever. No one can turn your open work into a closed commercial product.

When you share or use CC BY-NC-SA material, you should:

  1. Credit the creator — Name the original author (e.g., "AVI" or Academic Visuals Initiative)
  2. Link to the license — Provide a URL to CC BY-NC-SA 4.0
  3. Indicate changes — Say if you modified the original
  4. Link to the original — Provide a URL to the original work (if reasonably possible)

Example for attribution for AVI: Academic Visuals Initiative (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0)

Example for modified attribution for AVI: "Academic Visuals Initiative (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) | Modified."
